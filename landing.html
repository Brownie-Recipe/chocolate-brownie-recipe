Origins of the Name

The name 'Wambule' finds its roots in the ancient Kirati dialects—a composite of 'Wam' (Bear) and 'Dyal' (Village). It tells a story of survival and coexistence in the high-altitude forests where the black bear was both a physical neighbor and a spiritual ancestor.

language

"In the language of the ancestors, every mountain has a name, and every name has a soul."

Some scholars propose a purely descriptive origin, suggesting the name is derived from 'Vanbu'. When combined with the common Nepali suffix '-le', it denotes the people belonging to that specific region or lineage, focusing on geographical identification over mythic roots.

The Royal Theory traces the name to a legendary Kirat king named 'Vaizbu'. In this historical interpretation, 'Wamdyal' translates directly to the 'Village of Vaizbu', suggesting the community was founded as a royal seat or a protected settlement under his reign.

Jagrat: The Ancestral Eye

The primary religious group is the Jagrat, known as 'Sama pwaco'—those who obey the deities and spirits. Their faith bridges the human world with the Sama (ancestral spirits) while maintaining protection against the Hivaco Pudme (malevolent spirits) of the high ridges.

visibility

Libju (Gorujure Dada)

Grandfather Libju, the male primordial ancestor. Represented by the 1957-meter Libju Hill (Gorujure Dada) north of the Dudh Koshi, he is the benevolent protector offering spiritual strength to all clans.

grass

Bhumju

The female primordial ancestor, represented by the 1400-meter Bhumju Hill south of the Dudh Koshi. Her spiritual presence is anchored near Damli village, overseeing the fertility of the land.

The seasonal and spiritual cycles that define Wambule life.

Annual Harvest Festival

The offering of new crops to ancestors before human consumption, a ritual of deep gratitude for the land's bounty.

Ancestral & Funeral Rituals

A spiritual journey ensuring that the souls of the departed reach the ancestral realm through intricate chanting and prayer.

Worship of the Earth

A 9-day collective nature festival honoring the earth spirits, marking the beginning of the sowing season.

A mandatory ritual conducted at the ancestral shrine to feed and honor the spirits of family members.

Evolving Identity

The Reformed Sects

Santau-Bhes

A movement bridging the gap between Kirati shamanism and Hindu philosophy, emphasizing moral conduct and organized temple worship.

Hwam

A syncretic evolution focusing on internal purification and communal harmony, adapting ancestral traditions for the modern Kirat identity.
